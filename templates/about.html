PoultryScan AI — Automated Poultry Disease Classification

An end-to-end computer vision and deep learning platform engineered to screen for critical avian pathologies from digital imagery. Empowers poultry farmers, farm supervisors, and agricultural extension workers with rapid clinical decision support across head/face and body/droppings inspection.

Python 3.12 / 3.11 TensorFlow 2.16+ / 2.21 Keras 3 MobileNetV2 Transfer Learning 5 Verified Diagnostic Classes Flask REST Backend SQLite3 Database Vanilla CSS Design System

🎯 Problem Statement & Motivation

Avian infectious diseases (such as Coccidiosis, Fowl Pox, Newcastle Disease, and Salmonellosis) propagate rapidly through commercial and backyard flocks. Due to limited rural veterinary access, outbreaks often remain unaddressed until mortality escalates, causing severe flock loss and economic damage.

PoultryScan AI provides non-invasive, instant image-based screening accessible via mobile and desktop browsers to flag potential diseases early and suggest timely biosafety interventions.

📸 Dual Clinical Imaging Modalities

  • ✓ Chicken Head / Face Analysis: Inspects visible cutaneous lesions including Fowl Pox scabs on comb/wattles, facial edema, and cyanosis.
  • ✓ Full Body & Droppings Analysis: Evaluates plumage posture, cloacal vent feather staining, and pathognomonic fecal signs (bloody, chalky, or emerald-green).
  • ✓ Genuine Neural Inference: Directly connected to fine-tuned MobileNetV2 with dynamic softmax probability distributions—no hardcoded or simulated values.
  • ✓ Audit Trail Persistence: Every prediction is archived in SQLite (predictions.db) with timestamp, mode, confidence, and certainty status.

3. Classification Head

4. Uncertainty Engine

⚖️ System Purpose & Clinical Veterinary Limitations

Decision Support Only: PoultryScan AI is designed as a preliminary triage screening tool to help farmers and poultry technicians detect disease indicators rapidly. It does not replace certified veterinary laboratory diagnostics such as post-mortem necropsy, bacterial culture, or RT-PCR assays.

Action on Suspected Outbreaks: If the model identifies severe notifiable conditions such as Newcastle Disease or rapid flock mortality, immediately quarantine the shed, restrict visitor entry, and report to licensed avian veterinary practitioners and regional agricultural extension services.
